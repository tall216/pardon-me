# Places

Every location lists what it is **for** in a story, so the Director can pick by
need. Landmark relations are fixed; see the map note at the bottom.

## Uncle D's house

`CONFIRMED` Uncle D has a home in Quail Hollow.

`PROPOSED` A narrow two-story house painted a yellow that used to be brighter,
on **Pullet Lane**, two doors from the creek. The important part is the
**porch**: deep, sagging on the left, with two chairs that do not match, a
cooler that is also a table, and a rail where the quail sit. The porch is the
show's home base and where most episodes begin and end.

Inside: a kitchen with a radio that only gets one station, a hallway of framed
photos Winston has not been told the stories behind, Winston's room (the small
one, meticulously organized, upstairs), and Uncle D's "workshop" (the garage,
which has never held a car).

**For:** openings, endings, the quiet talk before the button, any scheme that
starts with "now hear me out."

## Mabel's Place

`PROPOSED` A diner and general store under one roof on **Main Street**, run by
Mabel Okafor. Six stools, two booths, a shelf of hardware behind the pie case.
The town's actual town hall; the official one is for ceremonies. There is a
corkboard by the door where every town announcement, argument, and lost-cat
notice is pinned, usually on top of each other.

**For:** gossip spreading, the whole cast in one room, B-plots, anyone needing
to buy one odd item at a bad time.

## The water tower

`PROPOSED` On the rim of the valley, by the only road out. Painted with the
town name and a quail that was supposed to look proud and looks worried.
Climbable, and forbidden to climb, which means it gets climbed once a season.
From the top you can see all of Quail Hollow, which is how characters realize
things.

**For:** the big view, the scheme that has gotten out of hand, the "we can see
it from here" shot, dares.

## Covey Creek

`PROPOSED` Runs through the bottom of the valley behind Pullet Lane. Shallow,
cold, full of crawdads and lost objects. There is a **swinging bridge** that is
safe and a **log** that is not. Winston uses the bridge. Uncle D uses the log.

**For:** kid adventures, lost-object plots, Uncle D falling in (once per
season, by tradition).

## Town Hall and the Mayor's office

`PROPOSED` A white building with columns that are slightly too big for it. One
room is the Mayor's office, which has a desk the size of a boat. The rest is a
hall used for the raffle, the pageant, the pie contest, and emergencies, which
in Quail Hollow are the same thing.

**For:** anything official going wrong, Mayor Pinkney, contests.

## The library

`PROPOSED` One room, one librarian (unnamed so far), one computer under a
sheet. Winston's favorite place in town and the only quiet one.

**For:** Winston alone, research montages, the computer joke.

## The Fairgrounds

`PROPOSED` A field past the creek with a permanent bandstand and a temporary
everything else. Hosts the Covey Days festival every season finale.

**For:** finales, crowds, anything with a ribbon.

## The Dead End

`PROPOSED` Pullet Lane runs out at the foot of the rim in a turnaround ringed by
pines. Teenagers go there to be dramatic. Uncle D goes there to think, which
Winston has noticed and does not mention.

**For:** the one serious scene an episode is allowed.

## Old Covey Road and "the Top"

`PROPOSED` The road out. It goes up past the water tower and over the rim. The
Top is the only place in the valley where a phone gets a signal, which is why
nobody is ever there when a call matters.

**For:** leaving-town threats, the phone joke, deliveries arriving.

## Map note

`PROPOSED` Fixed relations the Director must keep:

- Creek at the bottom, water tower on the rim, Main Street between them.
- Uncle D's porch faces east, toward Main Street. The water tower is visible
  from the porch over the roofs. The creek is behind the house.
- Mabel's is a two-minute walk from the porch. Winston times it. It is
  "one minute forty if you don't stop to talk," which Uncle D never manages.
