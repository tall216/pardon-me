# Rules of the show

Read in full before planning any episode.

## What the show is

`PROPOSED` Working title: **Uncle D**. A small-town cartoon comedy about a
relaxed, improvising uncle and the serious nine-year-old nephew who has come to
live with him in Quail Hollow. The engine of every episode is the gap between
how Uncle D thinks a problem should be handled (loosely, cheerfully, with a
plan that appears halfway through) and how Winston thinks it should be handled
(with a list, in order, before anything goes wrong).

`CONFIRMED` The show is drawn in the **Cartoony** style and built through the
Cartoony pipeline. Character sheets (starting with Uncle D's) are the visual
source of truth. The lore does not describe line weight, palette, or
proportions; it describes who people are and what they wear.

## Format

`PROPOSED`

- Episodes run **7 to 11 minutes**. A "double" is two linked shorts, never one long one.
- Structure: **cold open** (under 45 seconds, one joke, usually unrelated), title
  card, **A-plot** carried by Uncle D and Winston, a light **B-plot** carried by
  the supporting cast, a **button** that returns to the cold-open joke or to the
  porch.
- Every episode ends with the status quo restored. Houses rebuilt, grudges
  dropped, the quail back on the fence. Exceptions go in `continuity-log.md`
  with the owner's sign-off.
- One location per act where possible. Quail Hollow is small; the show should
  feel small.

## Tone

`PROPOSED`

- **Warm, not sappy.** Uncle D and Winston love each other and would rather
  eat gravel than say so.
- **Dumb things handled with dignity.** Characters take ridiculous problems
  seriously. Nobody winks at the camera.
- **Kindness wins, slowly.** The town is full of people who are wrong in
  cheerful ways. Nobody is a villain. Mayor Pinkney is the closest the show gets,
  and he is mostly just loud.
- **No meanness toward the audience's kid.** Winston is allowed to be right.
  He is often right. The joke is never "the smart kid is annoying"; the joke is
  that being right is not the same as being useful.

## Cartoony physics (what the world allows)

`PROPOSED` These rules are about *story logic*. The style pipeline handles the
drawing.

Allowed, and encouraged:

- Squash, stretch, and anticipation are real forces. A character can wind up
  before running and leave a dust cloud shaped like themselves.
- Falls, crashes, and explosions hurt for exactly one shot. A bandage or a
  smudge of soot is the maximum lasting damage, and it is gone by the next scene.
- Objects can be pulled from behind the back if they are the kind of object
  that character would plausibly own. Uncle D can produce a folding chair.
  Winston can produce a clipboard. Neither can produce a jet engine.
- Signs, labels, and newspaper headlines may narrate ("UNCLE D DOES IT AGAIN").
- Animals understand English and are judgmental about it. They do not speak.

Not allowed:

- No death, no real weapons, no blood. A "cannon" fires confetti or a bird.
- No character breaks the fourth wall. Signs may; people may not.
- No magic, no aliens, no time travel. Quail Hollow is strange enough on its own.
  If an episode seems to need magic, it needs a better scheme from Uncle D.
- No phones that work properly. Phones exist and are always the wrong tool.
  (See `world.md`.)

## Continuity rules

`PROPOSED`

- Nobody ages. Winston is nine forever. Seasons change; years do not.
- Uncle D's house, Mabel's Place, and the water tower are fixed landmarks and
  always stand in the same relation to each other (see `places.md`).
- Characters have a **default outfit** that is their silhouette. Alternate
  outfits exist for weather, occasions, and jokes, and are listed per character.
  An episode may invent a one-off outfit; if it is good, it gets added to the
  character's file.
- A character's history, once stated on screen, is logged and is true. Before
  giving anyone a new backstory detail, check `continuity-log.md`.
- Running gags are listed in `characters/_cast.md`. Use at most two per episode.

## How the Director should use this folder

- Pitch stories from the **wants** section of a character file. Every episode
  should put one character's want against another's.
- Pick locations from `places.md` by what the place is *for*, not by what
  looks nice.
- When unsure whether something is canon, treat it as `PROPOSED`, write the
  episode, and flag it in the episode file's "Lore touched" section.
- Never resolve an `OPEN` question inside an episode. Write around it.
