# Uncle D

`CONFIRMED` Uncle D exists, is a lead, and has a Cartoony character sheet. The
sheet is the visual source of truth. When it lands, its facts replace anything
below that they contradict.

## At a glance

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Full name | `PROPOSED` Delmar Ambrose Dupree. Answers only to "D." Claims the D stands for "Don't ask." |
| Age | `PROPOSED` Fifty-eight, "give or take, mostly give." |
| Role | Winston's uncle and guardian. The town's unofficial fixer of things that were not broken until he arrived. |
| Lives | Uncle D's house, Pullet Lane (see `../places.md`). |
| Voice | `PROPOSED` Low, unhurried, warm. Finishes other people's sentences with the wrong ending on purpose. Never raises his voice except to call across the creek. |
| Catchphrase | `PROPOSED` **"Pardon me."** Said right before doing the thing. Not an apology. A warning. |

## History

`PROPOSED`

- Born in Quail Hollow, in the same yellow house. Has left the valley four
  times. Came back each time within a week with a story that changes every
  telling.
- Was, briefly and by his own account only, a radio repairman, a
  minor-league groundskeeper, a sign painter (the water tower quail is his and
  he is sensitive about it), and "in sales." Nobody has verified any of these.
- Younger brother of Winston's mother, Celeste. They are close in the way of
  siblings who talk once a month and say everything in eleven minutes.
- Has lived alone since his own mother passed. The house is hers and he has
  changed nothing except the garage, which she never saw and would not have
  allowed.
- Took Winston in at the start of the show, without hesitation and without
  the faintest idea what a nine-year-old eats.

## What he wants

`PROPOSED`

- **Surface want:** for every day to be a good day, defined as nobody being
  upset at the end of it. He will go to enormous lengths to engineer this and
  cause most of the upset along the way.
- **Deep want:** to be the kind of person Winston's mother thought he was when
  she sent her son to him. He does not say this. He paints the porch instead.
- **Fear:** being found out as someone who does not know what he is doing. He
  has been found out roughly weekly for forty years and still fears it.

## How he operates

`PROPOSED`

- Plans appear mid-action. "Step one, we start. Step two is where it gets
  interesting."
- Fixes things with whatever is in reach. The result works and looks wrong.
- Never lies to Winston outright. He omits, reframes, and changes the subject
  with a snack. If Winston asks a direct question, D answers it, which is why
  Winston has learned to ask direct questions.
- Is genuinely good with people and genuinely bad with objects.
- Cannot pass Mabel's without stopping. Cannot pass the Dead End without
  slowing down.

## Relationships

`PROPOSED`

- **Winston:** adores him, is slightly afraid of him, treats him as a
  colleague. Calls him "Win" when things are fine and "Winston" when he is
  worried, which Winston has noticed.
- **Mabel:** oldest friend. They dated for one summer forty years ago and both
  describe it as "a clerical error." She is the only person who can stop a
  scheme with a look.
- **Mayor Pinkney:** D thinks he is harmless and funny. Pinkney thinks D is a
  menace to civic order. They are both right.
- **June:** D treats her like a second nephew and feeds her constantly.
- **The Covey:** mutual suspicion. The quail have seen too much.

## Lines he would say

`PROPOSED`

- "Pardon me." (then the thing)
- "Now hear me out."
- "That's not broke, that's just honest."
- "Win, write that down. We're owed for that."
- "I've left this town four times and it's never once let me."

## Lines he would never say

- Anything that makes Winston feel like a burden.
- "I told you so." He has never been in a position to.
- A straight answer about the water tower quail.
